## Independent assessment of global prices

Fix any access profile, mobility and effort pair in H, and write Delta=Q_i-Q_j. Intersecting the customer's location interval, the interval on which product i beats j, and the interval on which product i beats nonpurchase yields exactly

`q_i=max(0,min(1,(t+Delta-p_i+p_j)/(2t),(V+Q_i-p_i)/t))`.

Thus the displayed demand is valid before assuming coverage. In the rival's own location coordinate the same expression holds with -Delta. The difference between product utilities has nonzero slope because t>0, so price or quality equality cannot create a positive-measure customer tie.

The delicate part of L1 is its exclusion of other equilibria. I checked the following steps independently.

1. A fixed sufficiently small positive price guarantees positive revenue against **every** nonnegative rival price: both t+Delta and V+Q_i are positive. Equilibrium expected revenue is therefore bounded below by a strictly positive constant. Prices at or above V+Q_i yield zero. Revenue tends uniformly to zero as p approaches zero or the reservation choke price, so equilibrium supports are compact and avoid those endpoints. Continuity makes every point of the support a best response, including its extrema.
2. Let U_j be the largest rival support price. At an own price p greater than both 2t and U_j-t+Delta, competitive demand is uniformly below one. For a sufficiently small cut epsilon, every state with positive demand gains at least epsilon/(2t) units; a zero-demand state cannot lose revenue. The gain in each positive-demand state is bounded below by `epsilon*(p/(2t)-q)-epsilon^2/(2t)>0`. The strict distance from full demand permits one common cut size. Positive expected revenue implies positive probability of such states. Thus `U_i<=max(2t,U_j-t+Delta)`. If one upper support bound exceeded 2t, the corresponding inequalities for both firms would imply `U_i<=U_i-2t`, impossible. Both supports are therefore within [0,2t]. This step does not assume coverage or pure strategies.
3. At those support prices, V>3t and Q_i>=0 make even a firm's offer to the opposite endpoint strictly attractive relative to nonpurchase. Covered clipped revenue against any fixed r in [0,2t] has its unique maximum at `m_i(r)=(t+Delta+r)/2`. Its potential full-market segment ends below this peak because r+Delta<3t. Its interior segment is a concave quadratic, and its zero-demand tail is never optimal. This accounts for prices outside the support interval as deviations as well.
4. Against any rival distribution, a best response lies between m_i(L_j) and m_i(U_j). Moving inward from outside this interval improves revenue pointwise wherever revenue matters; a price with zero revenue everywhere cannot be a best response. Therefore each support width is no more than half the rival's width. Both widths must be zero. Positive revenue for both firms excludes a full-market/zero-market pure equilibrium. The interior equations yield `p_i=t+Delta/3` and `q_i=1/2+Delta/(6t)`.
5. At these candidate prices the rival alone covers the whole market. The candidate own price is the global unique revenue maximum, including all unilateral price deviations. This proves existence as well as uniqueness, without appealing to a grid or to an existence theorem for an unbounded action space.

The proof works at every feasible effort pair because H implies |Delta|<=D_max+E<t and Q_i>=0 throughout the box. No uncovered or clipped alternative pure equilibrium, nondegenerate independent mixed equilibrium, or tie-selection equilibrium is left within that domain. The compact proof in the producer document contains the necessary argument; the detail above records why its support step is sufficient, rather than introducing a missing assumption.

## Independent effort and access derivation

After the established price stage, own profit before the access cost is

`[t+(D+e_i-e_j)/3]^2/(2t)-c*e_i^2/2`.

Its curvature is `1/(9t)-c<0`. Differentiation gives the linear interior system

`(c-1/(9t))*e_i + e_j/(9t) = 1/3 + D/(9t)`

and the reversed equation with -D. Direct solution gives `e_i=1/(3c)+D/(B-2)`. Clipping the unconstrained response to [0,E] gives the global bounded best response, with Lipschitz coefficient `1/(B-1)<1`. Hence the joint best-response map has one fixed point; H places the solved point strictly inside the box. The expected objective against any rival distribution retains strictly negative curvature, so its unique best response is pure. This excludes nondegenerate independent mixed effort equilibria. Substitution independently gives the coefficients `K=t/2-1/(18c)`, `A=(B-1)/(3(B-2))`, and `C=c(B-1)/(2(B-2)^2)` in `K+AD+CD^2-ka_i`. The lower bound in H guarantees positive profit for every access profile in the sunk-entry model.

For access, the direct row comparison against S is `(K+A*delta+C*delta^2-k)-K`; against F it is `(K-k)-(K-A*delta+C*delta^2)`. These yield the reported g_S and g_F. Since H gives `|delta|<A/(2C)`, a negative delta makes both opening gains strictly negative for k>=0. At delta_P>0, R makes the smaller gain g_F strictly positive, and thus both are positive. This is a complete best-response comparison for the actual binary strategy sets. Strict dominance excludes mixed or asymmetric access alternatives at the two endpoint institutions. Unique continuations make this a subgame-perfect firm result, not merely a reduced-form Nash calculation.

At symmetric profiles these denominators give exactly the same per-firm shares, independent of mu. Outside mass is positive, and product participation is the outcome of a utility-maximizing choice, not a forced assignment.

At F/S, equal pooled H shares cancel in the quality difference, leaving delta_P. Under segmentation the opening firm's own menu loses some H share and yields delta_S. At symmetric profiles effort is 1/(3c) and price is t. ## Mobility, boundaries and manuscript use

For k>0, both gains are strictly increasing over the admissible delta interval. Their positive roots satisfy `0<delta_0<delta_1<delta_P`. Against rival opening probability x, the opening gain is `g_S-2C*delta^2*x`. In the interval between roots, this expression produces exactly two asymmetric pure equilibria and one fully mixed equilibrium. At the lower threshold, only the two edges with at least one firm choosing S surely remain; at the upper threshold, only the two edges with at least one firm choosing F surely remain. These are exactly the reported sets. For k=0 and delta=0, both firms are indifferent against every rival mixture, so every independent mixed pair is an equilibrium. The classification is exhaustive for independent strategies and does not claim to classify correlated equilibria.

The preserved failures follow directly from the equations: w=d=0 gives delta_P=0 and gains -k; w>=2vh/Z precludes customer harm when d>=0; high k defeats strict full dominance; large d can remove harm. Vanishing M or l and very large o eliminate the contribution wedge under the stated interpretation of limits. H explicitly declines to solve effort corners and low-reservation-value regimes outside its sufficient domain. The earlier strict-slope linear-demand computations, including the active effort bound and failed dominance at beta=lambda=0.5, remain unproved numerical evidence and are not upgraded here.
